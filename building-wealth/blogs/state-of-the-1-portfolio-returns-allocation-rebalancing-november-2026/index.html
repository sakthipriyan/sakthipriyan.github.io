What Changed This Month

Nasdaq 100 and Nifty 50 swapped sides. Nasdaq 100 went from +1.89% overweight to -0.41% underweight, while Nifty 50 went from the largest underweight at -1.24% to +0.77% overweight.

Total Drift narrowed from 3.30% to 1.97%. Last month’s investment was planned to bring it down to 2.27%, and market moves did the rest. The framework then did the opposite of last month: Nifty 50 went from receiving 54.96% of new capital to receiving none, and Nasdaq 100 went from none to 42.78%.

Definitions

  • Asset Class: Underlying asset class within the 1 Portfolio.
  • XIRR: Annualized return generated by investments in the asset class. Note: Asset-level XIRRs are based on the actual cash flows into each sleeve and therefore should be interpreted alongside the investment age shown in the table.
  • Growth Share: Percentage of total portfolio gains contributed by the asset class (Asset Class Gain ÷ Total Portfolio Gain). Note: This can be negative if an asset class has generated a loss.
  • Current Allocation: Current percentage of portfolio market value allocated to the asset class.
  • Target Allocation: Desired long-term allocation for the asset class.
  • Drift: Difference between current allocation and target allocation (positive = overweight, negative = underweight). Arrow colour: green = moved towards 0, red = moved away from 0, yellow = crossed from one side of 0 to the other.
  • Total Drift: Calculated as the sum of positive deviations from target allocations. A higher drift indicates the portfolio is further away from its target allocation. Conceptually, it represents the minimum percentage of portfolio value that would need to be shifted between asset classes to reach the target allocation precisely (ignoring taxes, transaction costs, and other practical constraints).
  • Capital-Efficiency Multiple: Growth Share ÷ Current Allocation. A ratio above 1 means the asset is contributing more to portfolio growth than its size alone would suggest.

Performance & Drift Observations

  • Overall Portfolio: XIRR eased to 14.43% (from 16.92%). Nifty 50 was the only asset class whose XIRR rose, from 0.50% to 1.82%.
  • Nasdaq 100 fell back. Its XIRR fell from 34.38% to 27.95%, and its allocation fell from 41.89% to 39.59%, even though the sleeve is still the largest source of gains.
  • XIRR also fell sharply in the smaller Indian sleeves. Next 50 moved from 11.79% to 5.73%, Midcap 150 from 8.10% to 1.37%, and Smallcap 250 from 27.99% to 14.22%. These sleeves are only 1y 4m old, so their XIRRs swing more than the older ones.
  • Nasdaq 100 and Gold together account for 49.94% of the portfolio’s current allocation, but have contributed 87.97% of its cumulative gains. The Capital-Efficiency Multiple (Growth Share ÷ Allocation) shows Gold at 2.35x and Nasdaq at 1.61x, while Nifty sits at 0.17x.
  • Total Drift Decreases: With the large Nasdaq overweight gone, Total Drift narrowed from 3.30% to 1.97%.

Last month’s investment was planned to leave Nasdaq 100 at +1.15% overweight. A month later it sits at -0.41%, without selling anything. This is why I follow perpetual rebalancing rather than react to a single month’s move.

Definitions

  • Asset Class: Underlying asset class part of the 1 Portfolio
  • Current: Portfolio allocation before new investment (as of Oct 30 EOD)
  • Pre Drift: Deviation from target at plan time (sum of positive values = total drift)
  • New Invest: Percentage of this month’s investment directed to each asset class
  • Post Invest: Estimated allocation after the investment
  • Post Drift: Estimated drift after investment
  • Target: Target allocation for the asset class
  • Total Drift: Drift is calculated as the sum of positive deviations from target allocations

Allocation Key Observations

The four underweight sleeves (Nasdaq 100, Next 50, Midcap 150 and Smallcap 250) all level out at -0.37%. Nifty 50, the fifth equity sleeve, is overweight and received nothing. For comparison, September brought four sleeves to a common level and October brought three.

The new investment was calculated using a Forward Water-Filling approach. Capital is distributed across underweight assets until the remaining underweights converge on a common “water level.”

Nasdaq 100

Nasdaq 100 received the largest share at 42.78%, but its drift only improved from -0.41% to -0.37%. With a 40% target, it needs a large share of new capital just to keep its weight as the portfolio grows.

Next 50 & Midcap 150

Next 50 (16.53%) and Midcap 150 (22.41%) continued to receive capital, moving from -0.46% and -0.54% to the water level.

Smallcap 250

Smallcap 250 received 18.27%, its first new capital since May 2026. It had been closer to target than the water level, and even-drift optimization kept new capital away until the others caught up. At -0.55% it is now the most underweight sleeve.

Nifty 50, Debt & Gold

No allocations were directed here as they are overweight. Their drift eases through passive dilution: Nifty 50 from +0.77% to +0.49%, Debt from +0.85% to +0.77%, and Gold from +0.35% to +0.21%.

Drift Correction History

Last month Nasdaq 100 received nothing and Nifty 50 received over half. This month that reversed. These percentages are not tactical allocation decisions or market forecasts. They are the mechanical output of the rebalancing algorithm after accounting for the portfolio’s current drift and the month’s available capital.

Transaction Costs

With Nasdaq 100 back in the plan, this month’s FX remittance and ETF purchase cost 1.05% for this month’s volume, excluding TCS. That is a one-time cost, so here is how it compares with holding the Nasdaq 100 through an Indian mutual fund:

The 0.39% annual TER difference covers the 1.05% in under three years, before counting the lower dividend tax. If my full monthly amount had gone to Nasdaq 100, the cost would have been about 0.72%, assuming the same brokerage.

The comparison assumes the local fund is open for fresh money. 3 Paths to Global Investing for Indians covers why local mutual funds aren’t always available and how Irish ETFs via LRS can substitute for them.
